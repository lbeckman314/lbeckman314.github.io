In 1930 most people who climbed Mount Hood went up the south side, from Government Camp to the summit by the route now called the South Side or Hogsback. The rest of the mountain, with its crevassed glaciers, rotten rock and walls of rime ice, was largely untouched. Over the next decade the Wy'easters changed that.

<blockquote class="pull">Ric Conrad credits members of the Wy'east Climbers with the first ascents of three of Hood's hardest routes: the Leuthold Couloir, the Sandy Glacier Headwall and the Eliot Glacier Headwall.</blockquote>

## The Sandy Glacier Headwall, 1937

On Hood's west side the Sandy Glacier ends against a steep, ice-plastered headwall that drops close to 3,000 feet onto the glacier below. Russ McJury and Joe Leuthold are credited with the first ascent in 1937. McJury already had the North Face to his name, climbed the year before with Bill Hackett.

## The Eliot Glacier Headwall, 1938

The Eliot Glacier, on Hood's north side, is the mountain's largest. Above it rises a headwall of loose volcanic rock and ice, about 800 feet of exposed climbing. McJury and Leuthold climbed it as well. Most sources date the ascent to 1938, though one route database gives 1935. A climbing guide still calls the route on the right side of the upper headwall the *McJury–Leuthold*.

## The Leuthold Couloir

From Illumination Saddle, on the west side of the mountain, a narrow gully of snow and ice climbs toward the summit. It carries Joe Leuthold's name, and Conrad credits its first ascent to the Wy'easters. Today it is one of Hood's best-known intermediate ice climbs. We are still looking for the date and the names of the first party.

## The Wy'east Route

According to his *American Alpine Journal* obituary, club president Everett Darr made the first climb of the **Wy'east Route** with James Mount and Ralph Calkin. The route climbs the mountain's southeast side.

## Bringing old routes back

The Wy'easters also revived routes that earlier climbers had pioneered and then abandoned:

- the **Wy'east Trail**
- **Cathedral Ridge**
- the **Newton-Clark Headwall**

Members wrote articles about these routes and led guests up them so other climbers would start using them again.
