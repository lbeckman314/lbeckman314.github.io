He was never formally a member, but no account of the Wy'east Climbers is complete without **Ranger**.

## Born under the porch

In the summer of 1927 a dog belonging to a Warm Springs family who were picking huckleberries near Government Camp gave birth under the porch of the **Government Camp Hotel**. Mr. and Mrs. Rafferty, who ran the hotel, raised two of the puppies: **Ranger**, an Australian shepherd mix, and his brother **Laddie**.

Ranger made his first recorded summit in 1928 with a party of Mazamas, and went home with a satin summit ribbon pinned to his collar. In the ten years that followed he is said to have reached the summit **more than 500 times**. He often joined any party heading up, and he also went out on patrol with forest ranger and snowshoe maker Ole Lien.

## On the register

On **May 3, 1931**, a summit register entry records five Wy'east Climbers from Portland reaching the top at 6:15 a.m. with three dogs: Ranger, Laddie and one named Wolf.

That October a party was caught by a blizzard at the summit and sheltered in the fire lookout. They got down on a 400-foot rope with Ranger leading. The next morning's *Capital Journal* headline read **"Dog Rescues Six on Mount Hood."** Ranger was also credited with saving lives in two storms in 1930.

Laddie was killed by a car on the new Mount Hood Loop Highway in 1932, and Ranger stayed away from roads after that. His last documented summit was a New Year's climb on **January 1, 1938**.

## The last climb

Ranger died on **July 1, 1940**. A group of Wy'east Climbers, Ole Lien, Jim Harlow, Ralph Calkin, Ida Darr, Ed Meyer and Paul Parker, wrapped his body in cloth, strapped it to a board and carried it up the mountain. Just below the summit they dug a grave with their ice axes and built a stone cairn over it. They recorded the burial in the summit register on July 7.

<blockquote class="pull">In 1946 Ralph Calkin and Jim Harlow came back to fix a bronze plaque to the cairn. Avalanches and icefall have since erased the grave, and the story is what survives.</blockquote>

## Remembered

Former Mazama archivist **Jeff Thomas**, going through the old summit registers, found dozens of entries that mention Ranger. In 2024 he and filmmaker **Ned Thanhouser** released the short documentary *Ranger: The Climbing Dog of Mount Hood*, which was selected for five film festivals.
