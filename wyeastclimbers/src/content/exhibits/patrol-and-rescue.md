In the 1930s nobody was responsible for rescuing people on Mount Hood. When a climber fell, got lost or was caught in a storm, whoever was on the mountain went to help, and that often meant the Wy'easters.

## Trained from the start

The club is remembered as possibly the first climbing team to require **first-aid training of every member**. Conrad writes that Wy'easters "assisted injured alpinists in the early to mid-1930s," years before any formal patrol existed. His book draws heavily on the memories of Wy'easter Hank Lewis to reconstruct the club's rescue and recovery operations, some of them for fatal accidents.

## Timberline changes the mountain

On **September 28, 1937**, President Franklin D. Roosevelt dedicated **Timberline Lodge**, the grand stone and timber hotel built by the Works Progress Administration on Hood's south slope. Skiers came in large numbers, and so did injuries.

## Founding the patrol

Wy'east president **Everett Darr** and **Barney Macnab**, captain of the Nile River Yacht Club, went to the U.S. Forest Service and pressed it to act. The Mount Hood Ski Patrol traces its beginning to **1937**, when Wy'easter **Hank Lewis** became the first patroller on the mountain.

On **March 2, 1938**, the patrol was formally incorporated with about fifty members. Macnab was president and Lewis patrol chief. Darr, Macnab and Lewis held national patrol numbers 18, 17 and 78.

<blockquote class="pull">The Mount Hood Ski Patrol is often described as the nation's first organized volunteer ski patrol, and it is still at work today.</blockquote>

## What came after

Organized mountain rescue in Oregon kept growing. In 1955 climbing clubs, ski patrols and sheriffs' offices formed the **Mountain Rescue Council of Oregon (MORESCO)**, and from it eventually came **Portland Mountain Rescue** (1977). Whether former Wy'easters took part directly is one of the questions this site hopes to answer.
