The Wy'easters climbed hard, and they also clearly enjoyed themselves.

## Fire on the mountain

Portland's **Winter Sports Carnival** wanted a spectacle, and the Wy'easters supplied one. Climbers hauled magnesium flares high onto Mount Hood. Each was rated at "a million candle power," and at a set time they lit them. Depending on the flare it burned for one to six minutes, and the light could be seen from the city.

## The New Year Laurels

Since at least 1916, someone has tried to be the first person on Mount Hood's summit in the new year. That means a midwinter climb, often through the night, for what is mostly bragging rights. Conrad credits the Wy'east Climbers, together with a few unaffiliated climbers, with turning the **New Year Laurels** into a real competition.

## The outlaws

Conrad's "outlaws" were climbers who belonged to no club at all: not the Mazamas, not the Hood River Crag Rats, not the Wy'easters. Some made first ascents or record-fast speed climbs of their own, and they often roped up with Wy'easters. Conrad names **Gary Leech**, **Bill "Smoke" Blanchard** and **Hubert North**.

## The Nile River Yacht Club

The club's friendly rivals were the **Nile River Yacht Club**, a Portland *ski* club with no connection to the Nile or to boats. The rivalry turned into a partnership. Wy'east president Everett Darr and Nile River captain Barney Macnab together started what became the Mount Hood Ski Patrol.
